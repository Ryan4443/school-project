Chapter 02 · Mode of occurrence

Veins & lodes

In igneous and metamorphic rocks, minerals fill cracks, faults and joints. Smaller occurrences are called veins; larger ones are lodes. Hot, mineral-rich fluids rise through these gaps and cool into solid deposits.

Silver is rarely alone. It is mined together with lead, zinc and copper ores.

Chapter 04 · Inside the atom

Atom 47

Silver has 47 protons and 47 electrons arranged 2, 8, 18, 18, 1. The lone outer 5s¹ electron moves freely, which is why silver conducts electricity and heat better than any other metal.

Protons47
Neutrons60 / 62
Atomic mass107.87 u

Natural silver is a mix of isotopes Ag-107 (60 neutrons) and Ag-109 (62).

Chapter 05 · Chemical behaviour

Extraction & tarnish

Extraction: a more reactive metal displaces silver from solution.

Ag₂S + 4NaCN → 2Na[Ag(CN)₂] + Na₂S
2Na[Ag(CN)₂] + Zn → Na₂[Zn(CN)₄] + 2Ag

Corrosion: in air, silver reacts with hydrogen sulfide and turns black.

4Ag + 2H₂S + O₂ → 2Ag₂S + 2H₂O

Chapter 06 · Uses, conservation & quiz

Use it. Save it.

  • Solar cells: silver paste carries the current.
  • Photography: 2AgBr → 2Ag + Br₂ in light (photolytic decomposition).
  • Cloud seeding: silver iodide (AgI) helps make rain.
  • Conserve: workable minerals are only about 1% of the crust, so recycle e-waste and old jewellery, and cut smelting waste.
