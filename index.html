02 · Mode of Occurrence of Minerals

Veins, Lodes &
Host Rocks.

Minerals are usually found in "ores"—accumulations of any mineral mixed with other elements. In igneous and metamorphic rocks, liquid and gaseous minerals are forced upward through cracks, faults, and joints.

Veins (Smaller) & Lodes (Larger): Formed when hot hydrothermal solutions cool and solidify inside rock crevices. Examples: Silver, Tin, Copper, Zinc, and Lead.
Fracture Host Rock Cutaway Open Lode · 80%

Chief silver ore: Argentite (Ag₂S), co-occurring with Galena (PbS) and Sphalerite (ZnS).

04 · Sub-Nanometer Quantum Dive

Inside Atom 47
& 5s¹ Electron.

Silver has 47 protons and 47 electrons arranged across 5 shells: 2, 8, 18, 18, 1 ([Kr] 4d¹⁰ 5s¹). Its single outer 5s¹ valence electron detaches easily into a mobile electron sea, making silver the best conductor of heat and electricity.

06 · Metallurgy, Reactivity & Corrosion

Extraction &
Sulfide Tarnish.

Ag₂S + 4NaCN → 2Na[Ag(CN)₂] + Na₂S
2Na[Ag(CN)₂] + Zn → Na₂[Zn(CN)₄] + 2Ag↓

MacArthur-Forrest Process: More reactive Zinc displaces silver from soluble sodium dicyanoargentate.

Atmospheric Corrosion (Tarnishing): Silver does not react with oxygen even at red heat, but reacts with airborne Hydrogen Sulfide (H₂S) to form a black coating of Silver Sulfide (Ag₂S):

4Ag(s) + 2H₂S(g) + O₂(g) → 2Ag₂S(s) [Black] + 2H₂O(l)
